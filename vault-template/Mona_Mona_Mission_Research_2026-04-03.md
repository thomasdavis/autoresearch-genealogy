# Mona Mona Mission: History and Context for the Noble Family

## Mission Overview

Mona Mona was a Seventh-day Adventist mission for Aboriginal people, established 1913 near Kuranda on the Atherton Tableland, 25 km from Cairns. It was the SDA Church's first Aboriginal mission in Australia, founded by P. B. Rudge and J. L. Branford. The mission primarily housed Djabuganjdji (Djabugay) people, though people were removed there from other locations including Yarrabah.

**Strong Signal**: George Noble, wife Molly, and child George were removed from Yarrabah to Mona Mona in 1914, one year after the mission opened. They were among the earliest residents.

## Dormitory System

Children aged 6 and older were separated from their families and placed in single-sex dormitories:

- **Girls' dormitory** (built 1913): four rooms, dining capacity for 200. Destroyed in February 1920 cyclone, rebuilt 1926. Girls remained in the dormitory until they married.
- **Boys' dormitory**: established c.1930s. Boys trained in farming and saw-milling.
- **Little Boys' dormitory**: operated in the 1950s for children under 10.
- **Senior Girls' dormitory**: completed 1951, occupied through 1955.

Conditions were harsh. Girls were locked inside 6pm to 6am nightly with no mattress, sheets, or pillows, sleeping on the floor in winter. Children were underfed. Severe punishments included imprisonment and head shaving for minor infractions. Sexual abuse occurred and was reported but ignored by superintendents.

**Moderate Signal**: Dorothy Molly Noble (born to George Clifford Noble and Molly Noble at Mona Mona) would have grown up under this dormitory system from age 6 until her marriage to Charles Joseph Davis in 1935. If born c.1915-1920, she spent approximately 15 to 20 years under mission control.

## Economy and Daily Life

Until 1940, the mission was nearly self-sufficient: growing food, cutting and milling timber. A saw mill produced building products. After 1940, soil fertility deteriorated and costs rose, making the mission economically unviable.

## Exemptions

The exemption system allowed Aboriginal people to live outside mission/reserve control under certain conditions. George Clifford Noble and Molly Noble held exemptions at Mona Mona in 1922. This is significant: it suggests they had some degree of autonomy within or near the mission, though the practical extent of that freedom under Queensland's protection legislation was limited.

## Closure and Dispersal (1962)

The last dormitory closed 31 December 1962. The Queensland Government wanted the land for a dam on Flaggy Creek. Residents were forcibly removed to Palm Island, Woorabinda, or dispersed to Kuranda, Mantaka, Top Kowrowa, Bottom Kowrowa, Koah, Mareeba, and Cairns. The dam was never built.

The closure caused catastrophic generational dislocation, disrupting Bama families and tribal cultural practices.

**Note**: Dorothy Noble Davis died in 1950, twelve years before closure. Sandra Anne Davis (born c.1938-1944) may have been at Mona Mona during the final decade of operation, or may have already been living elsewhere after her mother's death.

## Abuse Inquiry and Compensation

The Forde Inquiry (Queensland) documented widespread abuse at institutions including Mona Mona. Over 70 Mona Mona claimants were initially denied compensation because the mission was "unlicensed." After a six-year battle involving the Australian Human Rights Commission, claimants received compensation. The SDA Church issued a general apology but no formal acknowledgment of Mona Mona's specific abuses. Survivors pursued further legal action.

## Land Return

In 2010, the Queensland Government granted the local Indigenous community a 30-year lease over 1,600 hectares (4,000 acres) of the former mission site. The Mona Mona Bulmba Aboriginal Corporation and Buluwai Indigenous Corporation now manage community development on the land. Some Djabugay people and former residents have returned and built houses.

## Archival Records

- **Queensland State Archives (QSA)**: government records related to the mission
- **AIATSIS**: holds authority record for Mona Mona (NE Qld SE55-02), some mission manuscripts
- **State Library of Queensland**: Tindale Genealogical Collection contains family trees from Aboriginal communities including Mona Mona, mainly from the 1930s. This is a priority source for Noble family records.
- **Mona Mona Descendants PDF**: hosted by Buluwai Indigenous Corporation (buluwai.org), access was blocked (403). May contain Noble family information.
