# Sandra Anne Davis (Mabb)

**Sandra Anne Davis** (born 18 October, c.1938--1944; died c.1 April 2026, Far North Queensland) was an Aboriginal Australian woman of Butchulla, Quandamooka, and mixed heritage. She was a daughter of Charles Joseph "Chooka" Davis and Dorothy Molly Noble, and the wife of Kenneth William Mabb. Through her maternal great-grandfather Jack Noble (Wannamutta), she was a descendant of one of the Queensland Native Mounted Police troopers who participated in the Siege of Glenrowan during the pursuit of the Kelly Gang in 1880.

## Early life

Sandra Anne Davis was born on 18 October in an unknown year, estimated between 1938 and 1944, likely in the Mossman or Cairns area of Far North Queensland. She was the fourth child (or third surviving) of Charles Joseph Davis and Dorothy Molly Noble, according to the Thompson genealogy (version 3, 2017). Her birth year is bracketed by her parents' marriage on 2 October 1935 (QLD BDM reg. 1935/C/3890), her older sister Dorothy Elsa Rose, and her younger sister Gladys Joan Davis (born 25 April 1945).

Sandra grew up in a large family under the constraints of the Aboriginal Protection Act. Her father Charles Joseph held an exemption certificate from Mossman dated 1938, in which his parents' heritage was recorded as "H/C Phillipino, H/C Aborigine" (CIFHS exemption records 1936--1941). The family was based in the Mossman, Port Douglas, and Cairns corridor of Far North Queensland.

Her mother Dorothy Molly Noble died in 1950, when Sandra was approximately six to twelve years old. No record survives of Sandra's schooling, employment, or daily life during her childhood and adolescence.

No informal name or nickname has been found in any source for Sandra. This is notable given that her father was universally known as "Chooka," her grandmother Caroline Rose was called "Kaitchi," her aunt Gladys Dorothy Davis was "Glad," and her uncle Patrick James O'Shane was known as "Tiger."

## Parents

### Father: Charles Joseph "Chooka" Davis (1912--2007)

Charles Joseph Davis was born on 26 August 1912 and died on 24 February 2007 in Mareeba, Queensland. He was the son of George Edgar (Braikenridge) Davis and Caroline Rose Roberts/Brown ("Kaitchi") of Mount Molloy. George Edgar Davis was born between 1885 and 1890 and died on 10 March 1977; his birth father was recorded as John Davis.

Charles Joseph held exemption certificates from the Aboriginal Protection Act. Queensland State Archives hold three items of exemption correspondence for him: ITM3964021 (36/1263 Exemptions D-J), ITM3964022 (37/2717), and ITM3964020 (37/2976). A restricted item ITM3825160 records an exemption at Gunnawarra under agency control 38/469.

### Mother: Dorothy Molly Noble (d. 1950)

Dorothy Molly Noble came from Mona Mona Mission near Kuranda, Queensland. She married Charles Joseph Davis on 2 October 1935 (QLD BDM reg. 1935/C/3890). She died in 1950.

Dorothy's parents were George Clifford Noble (born c.1896) and Molly Noble (born c.1896), who both held exemptions at Mona Mona Mission in 1922 when George was recorded as aged 28 and Molly as aged 26. George and Molly Noble had been removed from Yarrabah Mission to Mona Mona in 1914, accompanied by their young son George. The reason recorded in the CIFHS Queensland Removals Register was "Interfering with dormitary girls."

Dorothy had at least two siblings: Agnes Norma Loretta Noble (born 1916, Mona Mona), who married a Pitt; and George Gregory Noble (born 1915), who married Alice Mona McKenzie in 1935 at Cairns.

## Maternal ancestry: Jack Noble / Wannamutta

Sandra's maternal great-grandfather was Jack Noble, born Wannamutta, c.1855 at Sandy Cape on K'gari (Fraser Island). He was a Butchulla (Budjula) man, the son of Jacko Morris and Carie Martin.

### Native Mounted Police service

At age 14, Wannamutta was removed from his family by Constable Thomas King on K'gari and enlisted into the Queensland Native Mounted Police (NMP). He acquired the surname "Noble" during his service in Victoria, reportedly given for his tracking abilities.

His postings included Rockhampton (initial training), Cooktown (arriving October 1873 aboard SS Leichhardt), Boralga on the Laura River (1875, under Sub-Inspector Stanhope O'Connor), Lower Laura (1879), Dunrobin (1886), Turn Off Lagoon near Burketown (1889--1901), and Musgrave (1902).

On 5 November 1873, Jack was present at the Normanby River lagoon massacre (Battle Camp), where between 80 and 150 Kuku-Warra people were killed.

### Siege of Glenrowan

In February 1879, Jack and five fellow Aboriginal troopers (Hero, Johnny, Jimmy, Barney, and Corporal Sambo) traveled to Victoria under Sub-Inspector O'Connor and Senior Constable Thomas King to pursue the Kelly Gang. Corporal Sambo died on 19 March 1879 in Melbourne from lung inflammation.

On 27 June 1880, after the murder of Aaron Sherritt, the party left Melbourne by special train. At the Siege of Glenrowan on 28 June 1880, the troopers positioned themselves in a creek near the Glenrowan Hotel and fired on the building. Members of the Kelly gang reportedly called them "little black devils."

Jack and the surviving troopers were promised 50 pounds each from the 8,000-pound reward for the Kelly Gang's capture. Descendants state this was never paid. In 1994, a descendant claim for $42 million (inflation-adjusted) was unsuccessful; the court ruled the Queensland government had received the funds on the troopers' behalf.

### Later life and death

After leaving the NMP following the Turn Off Lagoon incident (c.1889), in which he tracked escaped prisoner Joe Flick to Lawn Hill station and a gunfight killed Constable Wavell and an Aboriginal stockman, Jack returned to K'gari. At Bogimbah Mission in the early 1900s, he married Alberta Clift, a Quandamooka (Jandai) woman from Stradbroke Island, and customarily adopted her son George, who took the surname Noble.

On 3 October 1902, Jack and Alberta were removed to Yarrabah Mission near Cairns under the authority of Ernest Gribble. Conflicts with Gribble's wife Emilie led them to leave for Cairns, but Gribble prevented George from joining them. They returned to Yarrabah in 1903, where they sheltered mission absconders. In April 1906, they were moved to Myora Reserve on Stradbroke Island; George remained at Yarrabah. Alberta wrote letters pleading for her son's return. In 1926 and 1928, Jack was removed to Barambah (Cherbourg) Aboriginal Settlement.

Jack Noble died on 15 January 1935 at Pialba of kidney disease, aged approximately 80, predeceased by Alberta and survived by George. He has an entry in the Australian Dictionary of Biography. A dedicated page on his NMP service exists in the Frontier Conflict database.

## Paternal ancestry: Davis and Braikenridge

## Husband's family: the Mabb line

### Cecil Maurice Mabb (1908--1985)

Kenneth's father Cecil Maurice Mabb was born c.1908 in Hanwell, Middlesex, England. He was aged 2 on the 1911 ship manifest when his family emigrated to Queensland. He died on 26 February 1985 in Mount Isa (QLD BDM reg. 1985/52244) and was buried at Mount Isa Sunset Lawn Cemetery (Plot 1-1, Grave #1179, interred 4 March 1985).

Cecil married Florence Helen May Craig (born c.1910; died 9 June 1962; QLD BDM reg. 1962/C/1877). Florence's parents were Alexander Craig and Florence Theresa Mobey. Cecil and Florence had at least three children: Kenneth William Mabb, Rita Mabb, and Mabel Evelyn Gwendoline Mabb.

### Henry John Mabb and Susannah Underdown

Kenneth's paternal grandparents were Henry John Mabb (baptised 20 November 1870, Wadhurst, Sussex; died 11 January 1914, Queensland; QLD BDM reg. 1914/B/18825; parents: Richard Mabb and Frances Weaver; occupation: tramway motorman) and Susannah Underdown (born 1875, Matfield, Kent; died 24 December 1939; married 1894, Tunbridge Wells).

The family emigrated to Queensland aboard the SS Oswestry Grange, departing London on 15 July 1911, with ten children: Mabel (16), Ernest (15), Charles (14), Elsie (11), Beatrice (8), Fred (5), Stanley (4), Christopher (4), Cecil (2), and Phyllis (2 months). Henry died just three years after arriving in Queensland. Susannah subsequently married David George Felix in 1916.

### Leslie Victor Mabb and the FNQ connection

Kenneth's uncle Leslie Victor Mabb (born 19 December 1912; died 23 August 1978; buried Albany Creek Memorial Park, Brisbane) appeared on the Kennedy electoral roll in the 1930s, which covered the Cairns area. This places a Mabb family member in Far North Queensland a generation before Kenneth's marriage to Sandra, providing a possible explanation for how Kenneth came to live in the Innisfail/Cairns area.

## Descendants: Patricia Mabb / Jensen

Patricia Mabb, listed in the Thompson genealogy as a daughter of Sandra and Kenneth, is believed to be the same person as Patricia Mary Mabb, also known as Patricia Mary Jensen, whose probate notice appeared in the Queensland Law Reporter on 31 January 2025 ([2025] 4 QLR). The notice states she was late of Infinite Care Mount Lofty, Harlaxton (a 134-bed aged care facility in a Toowoomba suburb), and names the probate applicants as Noel Evan Jensen and Judith-Ann Pearce. Her will was dated 6 October 2010.

A funeral notice for Patricia (Pat) Jensen (Deaths and Funerals Australia, notice ID 637561, created 27 December 2024) confirms a service at 12:30 pm on Monday 30 December 2024 at Pinnaroo Cemetery, 285 Graham Road, Bridgeman Downs, Queensland. The funeral was handled by Burstows Funeral Care, Toowoomba. No family members were named in the notice.

Noel Evan Jensen is a solicitor at 112 Herries Street, Toowoomba QLD 4350, who founded Jensen & Co Lawyers in 1993. He is registered with the Queensland Law Society and is a Board Member of the Legal Practitioners Admissions Board. His firm biography notes he played at Roland Garros in the 1979 French Open, confirmed by an ATP Tour player profile. His role as probate applicant (not merely solicitor) suggests a personal relationship with the deceased, most likely as her husband.

The identity of Judith-Ann Pearce, the second probate applicant, is unknown. She may be Patricia's daughter, a Jensen family relative, or connected to the Mabb family through another line.

This identification remains at moderate confidence. No direct documentary link (marriage record, obituary naming parents, or family tree entry) has been found to confirm that Patricia Mary Jensen is Sandra's daughter.
