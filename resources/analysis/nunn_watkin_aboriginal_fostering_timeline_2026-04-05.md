# Nunn and Watkin Families: Aboriginal Fostering Timeline

## PURPOSE

Document the history of the Nunn and Watkin families at Rossville/Helenvale to establish when and how Aboriginal children (particularly Julia and Nellie) came to live with them.

### Key Question: How did Nellie end up with the Watkins?

After George Watkin's sudden death in January 1901, Annie was a widow running the Lion's Den alone with 6 children (ages 1-14). Nellie was born c.1900, the same year as Annie's youngest child Charles Ernest. By 1905, Nellie was 4-5 years old and associated with the Watkin household.

Possible scenarios:
1. Rosie left Nellie at the Lion's Den after Rosie's death (if 1904) or departure
2. Nellie was informally fostered by Annie Watkin, who already had young children of similar age
3. Nellie was working/living at the pub as a domestic helper (though 4.5 years is very young)
4. The Protector placed Nellie with the Watkins under the 1897 Aboriginal Protection Act

The 1905 report says the woman in charge was "in the habit of drinking too much." If this was Annie Watkin herself, the Protector's removal of Nellie may have been triggered by concerns about Annie's drinking. If it was someone else at the pub (a barmaid, a traveller), the Lion's Den was still the location.

### Key Question: How did Julia end up with the Nunns?

Julia herself said her mother died and the Nunn family looked after her. Fred Leftwich (Julia's son) said their mother "kept moving with the two girls around the Cooktown / Black Mountains area" before she died. Then "Julia took the Nunn name while sister Nellie went to another family."

This suggests:
1. Rosie (or their mother) was travelling with Julia and Nellie in the Cooktown/Rossville corridor
2. The mother died (or was separated from the girls)
3. The local white families took in the orphaned half-caste children: Julia to the Nunns at Nunnville, Nellie to the Watkins at the Lion's Den
4. This was informal fostering, not a government placement
5. William Nunn's death in October 1905 may have triggered the government removal; with the patriarch dead, the Protector may have decided the arrangement was no longer adequate

### The Timing Problem

Julia says she lived with the Nunns for "about two years." If removed in 1905 at age 9 (born c.1894/1896), she came to the Nunns c.1903. This aligns with:
- Rosie's alleged death at Pioneer Cemetery Cairns, 8 April 1904
- If Rosie died in 1904, Julia (age ~10) and Nellie (age ~4) were orphaned
- Local families took them in: Julia to Nunns, Nellie to Watkins
- Within a year, the Protector removed both to Yarrabah

But Julia also says she was "about 16" when police removed her, which would put the removal c.1910, not 1905. The 1905 date is from the Chief Protector's annual report and is more reliable than Julia's recalled age decades later.

    |

    |

The entire corridor from Helenvale to Bloomfield is about 40 miles. Everyone knew everyone. The Watkins packed supplies from Cooktown through Helenvale to Bloomfield. The Nunns ran cattle at Nunnville. Owen Reynolds drove bullocks along this same road for 20 years before settling at Cooktown.

## THE MORRIS DAVIES QUESTION

Morris Davies married Jessie Caroline Ross (b.1887), Annie Watkin's eldest daughter from her first marriage. At Annie's 1941 death, Jessie was listed as "Mrs M. Davis/Davies (Sydney)."

Julia's 1912 marriage record lists her father as "Davis." Julia was born c.1894 at Romeo, near Rossville. Morris Davies married into the Lion's Den pub family at Helenvale.

Could "Davis" on Julia's marriage record refer to Morris Davies? This is speculative:
- We don't know when Morris married Jessie (likely early 1900s)
- Morris could have been at Helenvale/Rossville when Julia was conceived (c.1893)
- But Julia's father is more likely a different "Davis" given the common surname
- The "Davis Hill" at Bloomfield, near Julia's birthplace of Romeo, is a separate lead

This needs further investigation: BDM search for Morris Davies marriage, and checking whether Morris was in FNQ in the 1890s.
