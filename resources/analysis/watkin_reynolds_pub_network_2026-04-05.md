# The Watkin-Reynolds Pub Network: Two Publicans, Two Orphan Girls

## THE CORE DISCOVERY

**Annie Watkin** ran the Lion's Den Hotel at Helenvale (Cooktown district) from 1901 to 1913.
**Owen Reynolds** ran the Family Hotel at Cooktown's Two Mile from 1900 to 1913.

They were concurrent publicans in the same licensing district for 13 years. Their licences appear on the SAME pages of the Queensland Government Gazette, year after year.

And two orphan girls, Julia and Nellie, were fostered by families connected to both pubs before being removed to Yarrabah in 1905.

---

## THE WATKIN PUB FAMILY

### George Watkin
- Licensed victualler, Lion's Den Hotel, Cooktown district, 1897 and 1900-1901
- From the 1897 Gazette (Vol 67, p.30446): "Watkin, Geo. ... Lion's Den"
- Described in OH6/180 (Dorothy Brown interview) as having taken over the packing business from a Scotsman
- Ralph Watkin (OH55_34) confirms: "Ralph's family owned the Lion's Den and had packhorse teams"

### Annie Watkin
- Licensed victualler, Lion's Den Hotel, 1901-1913 (18 consecutive gazette entries)
- Listed in 1906 Pugh's Almanac as "Helenvale, Mrs. A. Watkin"
- Described in OH6/180 as "old Mrs Watkin, who had the Lions Den hotel"
- Her son-in-law was **Morris Davies** (Dorothy Brown interview, OH6/180)
- Ralph Watkin's mother was a school teacher at Rossville and Shipton's Flat

### Norman Watkin
- Son of George/Annie. Continued the packing business after George.
- OH6/180: "Norman Watkin used to bring up the groceries, right up to where we were"

### Ralph Watkin
- Son (or grandson) of the Lion's Den family
- Interviewed in OH55_34 (28 Jul 1995) with wife Wilma Pearl Watkin (nee Bloom) and sister-in-law Louise Dean
- Described packhorse mail runs from Cooktown to Helenvale to Bloomfield
- Knew the Rossville area intimately

## THE REYNOLDS PUB

### Owen Reynolds
- Licensed victualler, Family Hotel, Cooktown (Two Mile), 1900-1913 (22 consecutive gazette entries)
- First entry: 7 Aug 1900, last: 5 Nov 1913
- The Family Hotel was on the Two Mile, the approach road into Cooktown from the south

## GEOGRAPHIC RELATIONSHIP

The Lion's Den at Helenvale sits on the road between Cooktown and the Bloomfield/Rossville area:

```
COOKTOWN (Owen Reynolds - Family Hotel, Two Mile)
    |
    | ~40km south on the Cooktown-Bloomfield road
    |
HELENVALE (Annie Watkin - Lion's Den Hotel)
    |
    | ~15km south
    |
ROSSVILLE / NUNNVILLE (William Nunn's station)
    |
    | ~20km south
    |
BLOOMFIELD RIVER / ROMEO / DAVIS HILL
```

The Lion's Den was the LAST pub before the Bloomfield wilderness. Anyone travelling between Cooktown and Rossville/Bloomfield passed through it. Owen Reynolds' bullock teams would have used this road for decades before he settled at Cooktown.

## THE ORPHAN REMOVAL: 1905

In 1905, two orphan girls were removed from Rossville to Yarrabah:
- **Julia** (age 9, born c.1894 at Romeo near Rossville)
- **Nellie** (age 4.5, born c.1900)

Julia went to the **Nunn family** (William Nunn's station at Nunnville, near Rossville).
Nellie went to "another family" - the **Watkin family**.

The 1905 Chief Protector report describes the removal. The woman "in charge" of the children was at a pub. Given the geography, this was almost certainly the Lion's Den at Helenvale, run by Annie Watkin.

## CONNECTIONS

### 1. Nellie fostered by the pub family
Nellie Watkin took the **Watkin** surname as her foster name. This means she was living with the Watkin family at Helenvale/Rossville. The Watkin family ran the Lion's Den. Nellie was literally living at or near the pub.

### 2. The pub was on Owen Reynolds' road
Owen Reynolds spent 20+ years driving bullocks between Cooktown and the Palmer/Laura, passing through the Lion's Den corridor. Even after settling at Cooktown and running the Family Hotel, the road through Helenvale was the main route south. If Rosie was at Rossville/Bloomfield in the late 1890s, Owen would have passed through regularly.

### 3. Morris Davies connection
Dorothy Brown (OH6/180) identifies **Morris Davies** as the **son-in-law of old Mrs Watkin**. "Davies" is a common Welsh variant of "Davis." Julia's 1912 marriage record lists her father as "Davis." Could the "Davis" who fathered Julia be connected to the Davies family who married into the Watkins? This is speculative but the name overlap at the same location is notable.

### 4. Both pubs on the same gazette page
Owen Reynolds (Family Hotel, Cooktown) and Annie Watkin (Lion's Den, Cooktown district) appear on the same gazette pages in every licensing period from 1901-1913. They were part of the same small licensing community. In a district with perhaps 10-15 pubs, these two families knew each other.

### 5. The Watkin packing network
The Watkins ran packhorse teams between Cooktown, Helenvale, and Bloomfield. This was the same transport corridor Owen Reynolds had worked as a bullock driver. George Watkin "took over the packing business" from a Scotsman (OH6/180). The packing and bullock driving networks overlapped.

## OPEN QUESTIONS

1. **Who was George Watkin?** He held the Lion's Den licence before Annie. Were they husband and wife? Father and daughter? Annie appears as "Mrs A. Watkin" suggesting she was a widow or married woman running the pub.

2. **How did Nellie end up with the Watkins?** Was she placed there formally, or did she drift to the pub as an orphan? Aboriginal children living around pubs was common in FNQ.

3. **Who was Morris Davies?** He married into the Watkin family. Is there any connection to the "Davis" listed as Julia's father on her 1912 marriage record?

4. **Did Owen Reynolds know the Watkins?** Almost certainly yes, given 13 years of concurrent licensing in the same district and Owen's decades on the Cooktown-south road. But we have no direct evidence of interaction.

5. **Was the Lion's Den where the 1905 removal happened?** The report says the woman in charge of the children was "at a pub." The Lion's Den was the pub at Helenvale, right on the route from Rossville to Cooktown (and thus to Cairns/Yarrabah). Annie Watkin was the publican.
