# Owen Reynolds and Rosie: The Story of How We Found Lisa's Great-Great-Grandfather

## The Short Version

Through DNA testing and historical research, we have identified **Owen Reynolds** (c.1845-1929) as Edgar's biological father. Owen was an Irish bullock driver and hotel keeper in Cooktown, Far North Queensland. He had a relationship with an Aboriginal woman named **Rosie** (also known by many other names), and together they had at least two children: **Edgar** (born c.1890) and **Nellie** (born c.1900).

This means Owen Reynolds is Lisa's 2nd great-grandfather on the maternal side.

The family had never known Owen's identity before this research.

---

## How We Know: The DNA Evidence

Lisa's Ancestry DNA test matches with descendants of Owen Reynolds' legitimate family. Owen had a daughter named **Catherine Reynolds** (1873-1939) with his partner Mary Connors. Catherine married Charles Patching in Cooktown in 1898, and their descendants (the Patching and Rawlinson families) have also taken DNA tests.

The key matches:

- **Kathryn Rawlinson** shares **149 cM** of DNA with Lisa across multiple segments, on the maternal side. Kathryn descends from Catherine Reynolds through the Patching family.
- **Scorpio88** (Kathryn's child) shares **127 cM** with Lisa.

These amounts of shared DNA are consistent with Lisa and these people being **half-2nd cousins**, which is exactly what they would be if Owen fathered both Catherine (with Mary Connors) and Edgar (with Rosie). Catherine and Edgar would be half-siblings (same father, different mothers), making their descendants half-cousins.

If a different man had fathered Edgar (say one of Owen's brothers), the expected shared DNA with Catherine's descendants would be much lower, around 20-40 cM. The actual amounts (127 and 149 cM) are far too high for that, and fit the half-sibling model precisely.

Ancestry's own **ThruLines** feature has independently confirmed Owen Reynolds as Lisa's 2nd great-grandfather, tracing the line:

> Thomas Reynolds (3rd great-grandfather, 1814-1895) and Anne Carmichael (1810-1896)
> ... their son Owen Reynolds (2nd great-grandfather, c.1845-1929) and Rosie
> ... their son Edgar Davis (great-grandfather)
> ... his son Charles Davis (grandfather)
> ... his daughter Janice Davis (mother)
> ... Lisa Davis

## Who Was Owen Reynolds?

Owen Reynolds was born around 1845, almost certainly in Ireland. His parents were **Thomas Reynolds** (1814-1895) and **Anne Carmichael** (1810-1896). Both parents died in Cooktown, where Thomas's gravestone survives (visible in Ancestry records). Going further back, Owen's grandparents were Thomas Reynolds and Kate Dwyer, and his great-grandparents were Ricd Reynolds and Margt Mulhall. The surnames Mulhall and Dwyer point to the Leinster/Midlands region of Ireland.

Owen had four brothers who all settled in Far North Queensland:
- **James Reynolds** (c.1839-1905) settled at Mowbray Vale near Port Douglas, became Chairman of the Douglas Shire Council. His descendants still farm the land today.
- **Patrick Reynolds** (died 27 July 1886) ran the Range Hotel on the Daintree. His wife Bridget Higgins continued the licence after his death.
- **Thomas Reynolds** (died 4 December 1886) was a cedar cutter on the Upper Daintree.
- **William Reynolds** took up Selection 128 on the Upper Daintree in 1882.

Owen himself was a **bullock driver and carrier** on the Palmer River goldfield track. His obituary (published in May 1929) described him as "among the first" at the Einasleigh and Etheridge goldfields and "one of the best-known bullock drivers." He later ran the **Family Hotel** at Cooktown's Two Mile (the road leading out of town toward the Palmer), holding the liquor licence continuously from 1900 to at least 1913.

Owen had **eight children** with his partner Mary Connors (they never formally married). His first child, Catherine, was born in 1873. His last child with Mary, James Conners Reynolds, was born in 1891. There is a notable seven-year gap in his children with Mary (1883 to 1890), which is exactly the period when Edgar was born.

Owen also had a child named **Michael Patrick Reynolds** (born 1905, died 1981) with a woman named Agnes. This proves Owen had relationships with women other than Mary Connors well into his later years.

Owen died on 2 May 1929 at the Cairns District Hospital, aged about 84. His body was sent by special boat to Cooktown, where he was buried at the Cooktown General Cemetery on 3 May 1929 at 5:30 pm. His obituary listed five surviving daughters and one son. The daughters had married men named Patching, Meldrum, Penny, Lee, and Potter. His sole surviving son was Owen Daniel Reynolds.

## Who Was Rosie?

Rosie is one of the most difficult people in this family to pin down, because she appears in the historical record under at least eight different names:

- **Rosie Homalee / Homarlee** (from her first South Sea Islander husband, Jimmy Homalee, from the island of Santo in Vanuatu)
- **Rosie Hippie / Hippi / Epi** (from her second South Sea Islander husband, Willie Api, from the island of Epi/Api in Vanuatu, which was spelled "Hippi" in Queensland records)
- **Rosie Julian** (from her third husband, Paddy Julian, an Aboriginal man she married at Mossman on 13 May 1949)
- **Rosie Honalle / Honalze** (a spelling variant of Homalee)
- **Rosie Brackenridge** (from the Brackenridge name used on her children's birth registrations)
- **Rosie Reynolds** (used in some family records and in Ancestry's ThruLines)

She was an Aboriginal woman. A 1942 government file describes her as "F/B or H/C Kanaka, about 60 years old" and records her birthplace as Mareeba. She had a brother named Tommy Gray at Mona Mona Mission.

### Rosie in the Government Records

Rosie appears in several official Queensland government documents:

**1901** (Walter Roth's Northern Protector report): A "Rosie" is described as "legally married to a kanaka at the Mossman" with children at Yarrabah Mission. This may be the same Rosie.

**1905** (Chief Protector of Aboriginals Annual Report, p.15): "Rosie Homalee, of Saltwater Creek, Port Douglas, who was left money by her late husband, a South Sea Islander, and who has over 30 pounds to her credit in the bank, has been allowed to draw 10s. a month up to 5 pounds for the support of herself and son."

**1906** (Chief Protector report, p.9, Mixed Marriages section): "Rosie Homarlee, an aboriginal woman, to a South Sea Islander. Cairns." This was a marriage permission, likely to marry Willie Api before the mass deportation of South Sea Islanders from Australia.

**1914** (Queensland Removals Register): "Half-caste daughter of Rosie and another" removed from Port Douglas to Yarrabah Mission. A daughter of Rosie was taken under the Stolen Generations policies.

**1915-1916** (Aboriginal War Census, QSA series S4354, page 138): **Rosie Honalle** is recorded at Port Douglas, on the same census page as **Fred Braikenridge** (one of Edgar's brothers) and a **Nellie** (no surname).

**1942** (QSA Indigence Cases): Rosie is recorded as "Rosie Homarlee EPI, widow of Willie API" at Mareeba. She listed no children in her application.

**1949** (QLD BDM 1949/C/1596): Rosie married Paddy Julian at Mossman on 13 May 1949, aged 57 (though her age in different records varies widely).

**1964** (QLD BDM 1964/C/3548): Rosie Julian died. Her father was recorded as "Mick."

## Edgar: What the Records Show

Edgar appears in the records under three names: **Edgar Brackenridge**, **Edgar Braikenridge**, and **Edgar Davis**.

**Birth registration** (delayed, 1906/O/924): Edgar Brackenridge, born 1890, father "George Brackenridge," mother "Rosie." This registration was done years after his birth, as part of a batch of four delayed registrations for Rosie's children (Edgar 1890, Joseph 1892, Frederick 1894, Arthur 1897). The father "George Brackenridge" on the birth record is NOT Edgar's biological father. The DNA proves a Reynolds man was the biological father.

**1904** (Chief Protector report, Yarrabah section): "A leaflet, The Yarrabah Times, is being published monthly. The compositors are two half-caste boys, aged thirteen and fourteen, Wilfred Brown and Edgar; the same two lads are also organists at the mission church." This Edgar, aged 14 in 1904 (born c.1890), was at Yarrabah Mission working as a compositor and organist. The age matches Edgar Brackenridge.

**1915** (CIFHS Exemption Register): "Edgar Braikenridge, Mossman, 1915." Edgar held an exemption certificate at Mossman from 1915, meaning he was freed from the restrictions of the Aboriginal Protection Act. This exemption was still active in 1947.

**1977** (Death registration): Edgar's death certificate names his father as "George Davis" and his mother as "Rosina." Neither name matches the birth registration (which says "George Brackenridge" and "Rosie"). The inconsistency in names across records is typical of Aboriginal families in this era, where European officials recorded names inconsistently and Aboriginal people were often identified by whoever they were associated with.

Edgar lived at Mossman for most of his adult life. He married and had children, including Charles Davis (Lisa's grandfather).

## Nellie: Edgar's Sister

Nellie was Rosie's other daughter, born around 1900. DNA evidence (through Hezron Murgha, 93 cM with Lisa, who also shares 43 cM with Owen's descendants) suggests Owen Reynolds fathered Nellie as well.

Nellie appears in the records as:
- **1915-1916 War Census**: "Nellie" (no surname) at Port Douglas, page 138, on the same page as Rosie Honalle and Fred Braikenridge.
- **1917 Removals Register**: "Nellie" removed from Port Douglas to Hull River Aboriginal Settlement.
- Nellie later married **Daniel Owens** in 1941 (BDM 1941/C/2960). Her descendants include Hezron Murgha (a confirmed DNA match to Lisa at 93 cM).

## The Reynolds Family Today

Through this research, we have identified living descendants of Owen Reynolds across several branches:

**Through Catherine Reynolds (Owen's daughter with Mary Connors):**
The Patching and Rawlinson families. These are Lisa's closest confirmed Reynolds-line relatives, sharing 127-149 cM of DNA. They are half-cousins.

**Through Owen Daniel Reynolds (Owen's son with Mary Connors):**
Owen Daniel moved to Townsville, married Elizabeth Aitken Cannon (from the prominent Cannon family of Cairns, after whom Cannon Park racecourse is named). Their son Owen Charles Reynolds married Mary Carmelia Rooney. Their daughter **Anne Marie Reynolds** (born 1946, Townsville) is a mathematics professor with a PhD. She is Lisa's half-2nd cousin.

**Through James Reynolds (Owen's brother):**
The Reynolds family still farms at Mowbray Vale near Port Douglas, 140 years after James selected the land in 1882. A fourth-generation farmer named Clint Reynolds works 300 acres between Port Douglas and the Daintree.

## What This Means

Owen Reynolds was a man of his time and place. He was an Irish immigrant, a bullock driver in the goldfields, a hotel keeper in a frontier town. Like many European men in Far North Queensland in the 1880s-1890s, he had a relationship with an Aboriginal woman. From that relationship came Edgar, and through Edgar, the family line that leads to Lisa.

The DNA evidence is clear: 127-149 cM shared with Owen's legitimate descendants through Catherine, confirmed by Ancestry's ThruLines algorithm. The historical evidence corroborates it: Owen was in the right place (Cooktown/Palmer corridor) at the right time (1880s-1890s), with documented Aboriginal contact (an Aboriginal servant named Billy who died at "Mr Reynolds" in 1896), and a seven-year gap in his children with Mary Connors that exactly overlaps with Edgar's birth.

No documentary record names Owen Reynolds as Edgar's father. The birth record says "George Brackenridge." The death record says "George Davis." But DNA does not lie, and the DNA says Reynolds. And of all the Reynolds brothers, the DNA amounts point specifically to Owen.
