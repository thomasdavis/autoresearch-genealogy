**Owen's pattern**: He had 10 children with Mary Connors from 1872-1891 (19 years). Then NOTHING with Mary for 8+ years. Then he fathered Nellie with Rosie c.1899-1901. He also possibly fathered Agnes Maud Reynolds in 1905.

**What was Owen doing between 1891-1900?** He was transitioning from bullock driving to hotel keeping. He was in his late 40s to mid 50s. His legitimate children were growing up. He still had constant contact with the Aboriginal community along his route and at Two Mile.

**Rosie's pattern**: After Edgar, she moved south to Mossman and partnered with George Brackenridge. She had children with him through the 1890s. But she returned to Cooktown in 1896 (hospital admission) and Nellie was born at Rossville (near Cooktown) c.1899-1901. This suggests she moved back north for a period, back into Owen's territory.

## George Brackenridge: The Man In Between

George Brackenridge is a fascinating figure. From GOKS:

- **Store owner at Mossman** - "a well known landmark of Mossman was destroyed when George Braikenridge's store was burnt to the ground"
- **Commercially active** for at least 12 years (1916-1928)
- **Plaintiff in court cases** - garnishee orders, obtained judgment by default
- **Had gold jewelry stolen** - "one gold ring, set with rubies, and one gold tie pin"
- **Married Ellen Jean Dubatoff** in 1921 - BUT this George Braikenridge (parents Maurice and Mary) is flagged as a SEPARATE person from the father on the birth certs

**IMPORTANT**: The George Braikenridge who died 1939 (parents Maurice and Mary) and married Ellen Jean Dubatoff in 1921 is explicitly noted as a DIFFERENT person from the "George Brackenridge" on the birth registrations. The store owner may or may not be the same as Edgar's registered father.

**The question**: Was "George Brackenridge" on the birth certs:
1. The Mossman store owner (European, wealthy enough for gold jewelry)
2. A South Sea Islander (the 1901 report says Rosie was "legally married to a kanaka")
3. A fiction - someone filled in a name on the delayed registration
4. Owen Reynolds using a false name

## The Two Periods: Why?

### Period 1 (c.1882-1883): Owen and Rosie
- Owen is 37-38, a bullock driver
- Rosie is 13-14, Aboriginal girl at McIvor River (on Owen's route)
- Power imbalance: European man, teenage Aboriginal girl
- Edgar born

### The Gap (c.1884-1898): Rosie with George Brackenridge
- Rosie moves south to Mossman with George Brackenridge
- She has children with him (Joseph, Frederick, Arthur)
- Owen continues at Cooktown with Mary Connors (last child 1891)
- 100km separates them

### Period 2 (c.1899-1901): Owen and Rosie Again
- Rosie returns to the Cooktown area (1896 hospital admission, Nellie born at Rossville)
- Owen is now 54-56, running the Family Hotel at Two Mile
- Rosie is 30-32, an experienced woman with multiple children
- **What brought her back to Cooktown?** 
  - George Brackenridge may have left or died
  - She may have been visiting family (Kuku Yalanji country extends from Port Douglas to Cooktown)
  - She may have been working in the Cooktown area
  - Owen's hotel at Two Mile, with its Aboriginal camp, created opportunity

### Key Difference Between the Two Periods
- **Period 1**: A teenage girl and a powerful European man. Likely exploitation.
- **Period 2**: An adult woman and an elderly man. Different dynamic. Rosie had agency - she had already had multiple partners, was married to an SSI, had children at Yarrabah. Coming back to Owen's territory as a 30-year-old was a choice, not exploitation of a child.

## Did They Have Contact During the Gap?

We don't know. But:
- Owen's bullock route passed through Kuku Yalanji country
- Mossman and Cooktown are both within Kuku Yalanji territory
- Aboriginal networks connected these places
- Owen may have known Rosie had children at Yarrabah
- Edgar was at Yarrabah from at least 1896 - did Owen know?

## The "Polly Reynolds" Question

A **Polly Reynolds** was buried in the Aboriginal section of Cooktown cemetery c.1902. If Polly was another of Owen's Aboriginal children, it shows a pattern: Owen fathered multiple children with Aboriginal women over decades, not just Edgar and Nellie.

**Agnes Maud Reynolds** (born c.1905, per Pioneers & Settlers Register) may also be Owen's child by a woman named Agnes - making Owen a father of Aboriginal children well into his 60s.
