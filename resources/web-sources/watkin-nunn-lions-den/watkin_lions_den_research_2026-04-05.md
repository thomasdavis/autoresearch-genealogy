# Watkin Family, Lion's Den Hotel, Nunn Family: Web Research Compilation

## 1. THE WATKIN FAMILY AT HELENVALE

### Annie Ward / Ross / Watkin (c.1852-1941)

The central figure. Three names across two marriages:

- **Born**: c.1852 (maiden name: Ward)
- **Died**: 2 December 1941, Cairns District Hospital, age 89
- **Buried**: Cooktown General Cemetery

**First marriage**: to John Hatley Ross ("Jack Ross"), 18 November 1885, Queensland
- Jack Ross was Welsh, from Rossville
- Together they opened the Lion's Den Hotel in 1875 at the junction of Little Annan River and Mungumby Creek (later called Helenvale)
- Named after the Lion's Den tin mine on the nearby tableland
- Jack died 31 December 1889, age 23 (some sources say age 23, which conflicts with 1860 birth)

**Children from first marriage (Ross)**:
1. **Jessie Caroline Ross** (b. 11 Aug 1887, Rossville) -- later married a Davies (likely Morris Davies)
2. **John Hatley Ross Jr.** (b. 12 Jun 1890, Rossville; d. 26 Dec 1915)

**Second marriage**: to **George Watkin** (b.1867), 27 June 1892, Far North Queensland
- George held Lion's Den licence 1897 and 1900-1901
- George died suddenly 10 January 1901 in Cooktown, four months after his last child was born
- George also ran a packing/packhorse business (took over from a Scotsman per OH6/180 oral history)

**Children from second marriage (Watkin)**:
3. **William Edward Watkin** (b. 18 Apr 1893, Cooktown)
4. **George Henry Watkin** (b. 17 Jan 1895, Helenvale)
5. **Annie Louisa Watkin** (b. 20 Aug 1897, FNQ) -- married **Ambrose Dowling**, 8 Dec 1918. "Mr. J. Davies" was best man.
6. **Charles Ernest Watkin** (b. 3 Sep 1900, FNQ) -- married Lilla Cunningham, 7 Oct 1933, Sydney

**Grandchildren (documented)**: George, Norman, Mavis, Ralph, Stan (all Helenvale)

**Annie ran the Lion's Den solo from 1901 to 1941** (40 years after George's death).

### George Watkin (1867-1901)
- Husband of Annie
- Licensed victualler, Lion's Den Hotel, 1897 and 1900-1901
- Also a packer (packhorse team operator)
- OH6/180 (Dorothy Brown interview): "took over the packing business from a Scotsman"
- Died suddenly 10 January 1901 in Cooktown

### Norman Watkin
- Son of George/Annie (likely one of the four named sons)
- Continued the packing business after George
- OH6/180: "Norman Watkin used to bring up the groceries, right up to where we were"
- Broke his right arm when his horse fell (newspaper record)
- Described as "the last packer" by Ralph Watkin (OH55_34)

### Ralph Watkin
- Grandson of George and Annie
- Interviewed 28 Jul 1995 for SLQ Bloomfield River Oral History Project (OH55_34)
- Married Wilma Pearl Bloom
- Described packhorse mail runs: Cooktown to Helenvale by motorcar, then packhorse from Helenvale to Bloomfield
- His father was George (a son of the original George), and his grandfather also packed
- Confirmed: "My grandfather... he used to ride to the big Annan bridge... then they'd go to Cooktown. 2 day pack horses there... he had a paddock and a hut and a shed there at beside the big Annan."
- His mother was a school teacher at Rossville and Shipton's Flat

### Morris Davies / Jessie Ross Davies
- OH6/180 identifies **Morris Davies** as the **son-in-law of old Mrs Watkin**
- This means Morris Davies married **Jessie Caroline Ross** (Annie's eldest daughter, b. 1887 Rossville)
- WikiTree lists one of Annie's surviving daughters at her 1941 death as "Mrs M. Davis/Davies (Sydney)"
- The Watkin-Davies connection is interesting given that "Davis" appears as Julia's father's name on her 1912 marriage record at Yarrabah

### Ambrose Dowling / Annie Louisa Watkin
- Married 8 December 1918
- "Mr. J. Davies" was best man at this wedding (likely related to Morris Davies)
- Another surviving daughter at Annie's 1941 death: "Mrs A. Dowling (Cairns)"

## 2. THE LION'S DEN HOTEL

### Timeline of Ownership
| Period | Licensee | Notes |
|--------|----------|-------|
| 1875 | Jack Ross & Annie Ross | Founded at junction of Little Annan River and Mungumby Creek |
| 1889-1892 | Annie Ross (widow) | Jack died Dec 1889 |
| 1892-1897 | George Watkin / Annie Watkin | George's first licence entry: 1897 Gazette |
| 1897-1901 | George Watkin | Licensed victualler |
| 1901-1941 | Annie Watkin | George died Jan 1901; Annie ran it for 40 years |
| 1941-1964 | Watkin family descendants | Annie died Dec 1941 |
| 1964-present | Various owners | Left Watkin family after 89 years |
| Current | Judith Fry | Owned 10+ years; rebuilt after Cyclone Jasper (Dec 2023) |

### Physical Description
- "A long low building hidden amid huge mango trees"
- On the banks of the Annan River, Helenvale
- 21 miles (~34 km) from Cooktown
- Single-storey timber and corrugated iron construction
- Famous for visitor signatures on walls
- Miners left pay tallies on the walls

### Key Events
- **1907**: Annan River flooding got into the bar of Watkin's Hotel
- **1936**: Governor Sir Leslie Orme Wilson visited; "met Mrs Annie Watkin of Helenvale, who has lived at Helenvale for the past 40 years"
- **April 1939**: Annan River flood -- highest water in 50 years. Annie (age 87), her son, daughter-in-law, and grandchildren spent the night on the pub roof. Three feet of water inside.
- **1940**: Annie involved in fraud case -- identified defendant Evan William Gough from memory
- **October 2024**: 150th anniversary celebration

### The Name
Named after the Lion's Den tin mine, itself named after a stowaway named Daniel who came to work there. The mine owner joked about "Daniel in the Lion's Den."

## 3. THE NUNN FAMILY AT NUNNVILLE

### William Nunn (c.1820-1905)
- **Born**: c.1820 (location unknown)
- **Died**: 15 October 1905, North Queensland, age ~85
- **Occupation**: Grazier
- "Pioneer of the Cook district"
- February 1881: applied for second-class pastoral land at Mt Esk (Cooktown Commissioner hearing)
- 1903 newspaper: "Mr W. Nunn... is 78 years of age" and "still a vigorous and energetic worker"
- Modest estate at death: realty GBP 341, personalty GBP 21

### Caroline (Wood) Nunn
- **Married**: William Nunn, 12 September 1874, Queensland
- **Born**: c.1856 (William was ~54 at marriage, Caroline ~18 -- 36 year age gap)
- **Role**: Sole executrix of William's will
- Probate granted 26 December 1914
- Property: Portion 26v, County of Banks, Parish of Monkhouse, North Queensland

### Violet Adelaide Nunn Heaslop (b. 9 Mar 1890)
- Daughter of William and Caroline
- Born Queensland
- Married George Irving Heaslop
- Inherited "Nunnville Acres"

### Nunnville Station
- William Nunn's property near Rossville/Cooktown
- "Nunn's Forks" is where Wallaby Creek flows into the Annan River, named after him
- Located approximately 2 km north of Helenvale (called "the Big Forks")
- Adjacent to "the old homestead of William Nunn"

### Connection to Julia and Nellie
- Julia used the surname "Nunn" at her 1912 Yarrabah marriage
- Julia's marriage record: "Julia Nunn, of Yarrabah, Spinster, Born at Romeo, Age 18. Father: Davis. Mother: Rosie Rosie."
- This means Julia lived with or was fostered by the Nunn family at Nunnville near Rossville before her 1905 removal
- Julia (born c.1894 at Romeo near Rossville) and Nellie (born c.1900 at Rossville) were removed together as orphans in 1905

## 4. THE 1905 REMOVAL AND CONNECTIONS TO PUBS/STATIONS

### Chief Protector Report 1905
Two orphan girls removed from Rossville to Yarrabah:
- **Julia** (age 9, born c.1894-1896 at Romeo near Rossville) -- took surname **Nunn** (from Nunn family at Nunnville)
- **Nellie** (age 4.5, born c.1900-1901 at Rossville) -- took surname **Watkin** (from Watkin family at Lion's Den)

The report states about Nellie: "The woman in charge of her was stated to have been 'in the habit of drinking too much community.' Sent on the Minister's order to Yarrabah."

The mention of drinking and a pub context strongly suggests the woman was at or near the Lion's Den Hotel, run by Annie Watkin.

### 1915 Removal of 13 Children from Rossville/China Camp/Bloomfield
In late 1915, 13 Aboriginal children aged 5-17 were removed from Bloomfield, China Camp, and Rossville to Yarrabah. China Camp miners George Waldon and WC Knowlton complained but were overruled.

This shows a pattern of child removal from the Rossville/Bloomfield corridor.

## 5. OWEN REYNOLDS AND THE WATKIN CONNECTION

### Owen Reynolds at the Family Hotel, Cooktown
- Licensed victualler, Family Hotel, Cooktown (Two Mile), 1900-1913
- First gazette entry: 7 Aug 1900; last: 5 Nov 1913
- 22 consecutive gazette entries over 13 years

### Geographic Overlap
Owen Reynolds and Annie Watkin were concurrent publicans in the same licensing district (Cooktown) for 13 years (1901-1913). Their licences appear on the SAME pages of the Queensland Government Gazette.

The Family Hotel was at the Two Mile (southern approach to Cooktown). The Lion's Den was 40 km south at Helenvale. The road between them was the main route south to Rossville, Bloomfield, and beyond.

Owen Reynolds spent 20+ years driving bullocks on this road before becoming a publican. The Watkins ran packhorse teams on the same road. These transport networks overlapped.

## 6. BLOOMFIELD RIVER ORAL HISTORY PROJECT (OH55)

### Watkin Interviews
- **OH55_34**: Ralph Watkin, Wilma Watkin, and Louise Dean, 28 Jul 1995
- Ralph describes packhorse operations in detail
- His grandfather (George) packed from the big Annan bridge to Cooktown (2-day trips)
- His brother Norman was "the last packer"
- Ralph himself ran the packhorse mail several times

### Other Relevant Interviews
- **Bob Harlow** (OH55_14): Ten Mile Station cattle; "Lion's Den Hotel stories"
- **Henry Walker** (OH55_27): Aboriginal; "Rossville childhood"; tree kangaroo hunting; Woorabinda placement
- **Ruby Friday** (OH55_20): Aboriginal; "half-caste" experience; Shipton's Flat hiding
- **Harry Shipton** (OH55_25): Aboriginal; Shipton's Flat birth; mission avoidance
- **Daureen Jones** (OH55_12): Kuku Nyungkul; missionary foster parents; Wujal Wujal
