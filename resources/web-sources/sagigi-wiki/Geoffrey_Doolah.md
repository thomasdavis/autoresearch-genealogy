# Geoffrey Doolah

## Identity

Born 8 June 1931 (per research request; not independently confirmed in web searches). Torres Strait Islander man, WWII evacuee from Thursday Island, who became a significant figure at Cherbourg Aboriginal settlement in Queensland.

Also referred to as "Dato Geoffrey Doolah" in historical records.

## WWII Evacuation to Cherbourg

Geoffrey Doolah was an evacuee from Thursday Island during the Second World War. In 1942, around 200 "coloured citizens" were evacuated from Thursday Island in Torres Strait and were temporarily housed at Cherbourg. While many evacuees eventually returned, Geoffrey Doolah stayed on at Cherbourg after the war.

## Life at Cherbourg

### Teaching

Geoffrey Doolah taught at **Cherbourg Mission State School in 1959**. A photograph of "Dato Geoffrey Doolah teaching at Cherbourg Mission State School 1959" is held in the Cherbourg Memory archive and has been published in academic research (ResearchGate).

### Dormitory Supervision

He served as a dormitory supervisor at Cherbourg, in addition to his teaching role.

### Music

Geoffrey Doolah was a talented musician who **played the banjo** and performed in many concert parties at Cherbourg.

### Arts and Crafts

Doolah was involved in setting up the Aboriginal arts and crafts and shell display for the **Native Affairs Department** at the Cherbourg Aboriginal Community in **1958**. This role connected Torres Strait Islander artistic traditions with the broader Cherbourg community.

## Legacy

According to the Cherbourg Memory website, Doolah was a major figure at Cherbourg, yet his story remains relatively unknown. The Cherbourg Memory project preserves photographs, oral histories, and documents related to life at the Cherbourg settlement, including records featuring Geoffrey Doolah.

## Context: Cherbourg

Cherbourg is an Aboriginal community approximately 260 km northwest of Brisbane. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander families from all over Queensland and the Northern Territory were forcibly taken to Cherbourg under the Aboriginal Protection Act. The WWII evacuation of Thursday Islanders added a Torres Strait Islander population to this already diverse community.
