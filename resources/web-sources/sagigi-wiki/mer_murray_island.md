# Mer (Murray Island)

**Mer**, known in English as Murray Island, is a volcanic island in the eastern Torres Strait and the principal homeland of the **Meriam people**. It is internationally famous as the subject of **Mabo v Queensland (No 2)** (1992), the landmark High Court of Australia decision that overturned the doctrine of terra nullius and recognised native title.

## Geography

Mer is a basaltic island formed from an extinct volcano, last active over a million years ago when the Indo-Australian Plate slid over the East Australia hotspot. It is the easternmost island in the Torres Strait, located approximately 225 kilometres from Thursday Island.

Key geographic features:

- **Terrain**: Rises to a plateau approximately 80 metres above sea level
- **Highest point**: Gelam Paser (230 metres), the western end of the volcano crater
- **Named after**: Captain Edwards of HMS Pandora named it "Murray Island" in 1791
- **Satellite islands**: Mer forms the Murray Group together with Dauar and Waier

The island's volcanic soil is fertile, supporting the agricultural traditions central to Meriam culture. The surrounding reef system provides rich marine resources.

## The Eight Tribes

Mer is home to eight tribes (clans), each with clearly defined territorial boundaries that begin on the land and extend out to the edge of the home reef. Until the 1890s, the clans lived in clusters of villages within these territories across the islands of Mer, Dauar, and Waier.

All eight tribes share the **shark** as a collective protective totem.

### Land Tenure

Within each clan, "nameholders" maintain ownership of particular plots of land, reefs, lagoons, and stone fish-traps. They are responsible for these resources on behalf of their family. This system of clearly defined, clan-based land ownership was central to the evidence presented in the Mabo case.

### Cultural Division: Garden People and Fish People

A fundamental division exists between the eastern and western clans:

- **Eastern clans (Samsep, Piadram)**: "Garden people" whose primary orientation is agricultural. In Malo ceremonies, they serve as **dancers**.
- **Western clans**: "Fish groupings" oriented toward the sea. In Malo ceremonies, they serve as **song men**.

This complementary division structures both economic life and ceremonial practice.

## Traditional Culture

### The Law of Malo

Before Christianity, the Meriam followed **Malo**, a body of law and ceremony with principles and structure similar to Christianity. According to tradition, Malo was one of four brothers who sailed from New Guinea. A strong wind separated them, and Malo sailed alone until a big wave sank his canoe at Mer. After arriving, Malo gave the Meriam their laws and ceremonies.

The **Malo-Bomai** religious and ceremonial system governed:
- Social behaviour and inter-clan relations
- Land use and resource management
- Dispute resolution
- Ceremonial obligations

The structural parallels between Malo and Christianity facilitated the Meriam people's adoption of the new religion after 1871.

### Agriculture

Gardening takes priority in Meriam subsistence. Fathers transmit gardening techniques to sons across generations. The volcanic soil of Mer is highly fertile, supporting a rich agricultural tradition that distinguishes the Meriam from many other Torres Strait Islander groups.

### Sea Rights

Each Meriam family holds sea rights and maintains:
- Stone fish-weirs on the reefs
- Crayfish holes on specific reef sections
- Lagoon territories for fishing

### Creation Stories

- **Gelam**: A boy whose journey created the three islands of Mer, Waier, and Dauar. The highest point on Mer, Gelam Paser, is named after him.
- **Nageg and Geigi**: Mother and son who transformed into triggerfish and trevally, explaining sea country's spiritual importance.

### Tagai (Stars)

The clans of Mer are connected to the **Stars of Tagai**, a constellation-based knowledge system that links social structure to celestial navigation and seasonal patterns.

## The Coming of the Light

Christianity arrived in the eastern Torres Strait when the London Missionary Society reached Erub (Darnley Island) on 1 July 1871. The faith spread rapidly to Mer. Today, 1 July is celebrated annually across all Torres Strait communities. On Mer, the transition from Malo to Christianity was relatively smooth due to the structural similarities between the two systems.

## Scientific Documentation

In **1898**, Alfred Court Haddon led the **Cambridge University expedition** to the Torres Strait, spending seven months documenting Islander culture. The expedition produced the first sound recordings and films of Torres Strait Islander people. Haddon's detailed ethnographic work provides much of the baseline documentation of Meriam clan structure, totems, and ceremonies.

## The Mabo Case

### Background

On **20 May 1982**, five Meriam men lodged a case with the High Court of Australia for legal ownership of Mer:

1. **Eddie Koiki Mabo** (1936-1992, lead plaintiff)
2. **Reverend David Passi**
3. **Sam Passi**
4. **Celuia Mapo Salee**
5. **James Rice**

### The Proceedings

Over 10 years, 33 Meriam people generated 4,000 pages of transcripts of evidence. The evidence proved that the eight clans had occupied clearly defined territories on Mer for hundreds of years and demonstrated the continuity of Meriam custom, land ownership, and governance.

### The Decision (3 June 1992)

Six of the seven High Court justices ruled in favour of the Meriam. The decision:

- Recognised the traditional ownership of the Meriam people over the lands of Mer
- **Rejected terra nullius**, the legal fiction that Australia was unowned at British colonisation
- Established that native title existed by virtue of traditional customs and laws
- Held that rights arising under native title were recognised within the common law

Eddie Mabo died on 21 January 1992, five months before the decision was handed down.

### Legacy

The Mabo decision led directly to the **Native Title Act 1993**, providing the framework for all Indigenous Australians to make native title claims. By 2022, over 500 successful claims had been made, with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander rights recognised across 40% of the Australian land mass.

## Government Control

- **By 1912**: 1,200 acres of Mer gazetted as an Aboriginal reserve
- **1936**: Mer's Chief Councillor Marou Mimi led resistance during the Torres Strait strike, with 70% of the Islander workforce participating
- **1939**: Torres Strait Islanders Act recognised Islanders as separate from Aboriginal Australians
- **December 2012**: Mer finally accepted a Deed of Grant in Trust for the islands of Mer, Waier, and Dauar, having initially declined on the grounds that the land was not the government's to give

## World War II

Meriam men served in the Torres Strait Light Infantry Battalion, receiving one-third of white servicemen's pay. Following a 1943 strike, pay was increased to two-thirds. Full back pay was offered in the 1980s.
