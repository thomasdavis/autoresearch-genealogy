# Badu Island

**Badu Island** (also known as Mulgrave Island) is one of the largest islands in the Torres Strait, located approximately 60 kilometres north of Thursday Island, Queensland, Australia. The island covers approximately 101 square kilometres and is the homeland of the **Badulgal people**, Torres Strait Islanders of Melanesian descent.

## Geography

Badu Island is roughly circular in form, approximately 6 kilometres in diameter. The terrain is rocky and sparsely wooded, with dense mangroves fringing the northern section. The surrounding waters experience complex tides reaching up to 7 knots. Moa Island lies to the west, separated by a narrow channel.

The island features multiple named headlands including Zigini Point, Rugged Point, Red Point, and Coconut Point along its coastline. A 500-metre airstrip serves the island's transportation needs.

## Communities

### Wakaid

Wakaid is the only town on Badu, located on the southeast coast. The settlement also includes **Dogai**, situated on the southeastern coast facing Moa Island, which serves as a residential area with housing and amenities. The town takes its name from the pearl lugger Wakaid, captained by Tanu Nona, which won government productivity competitions.

The population declined between 2016 and 2021, reflecting the broader trend of Torres Strait Islanders migrating to the mainland for employment, education, and services.

## History

### Pre-Contact Period

Before European contact, the Badulgal maintained village-based communities focused on hunting, fishing, farming, canoe building, and inter-island trade. The Badulgal also engaged in warfare, including headhunting, practices that persisted until the 1870s.

### European Contact

- **1605**: Willem Janszoon sailed near Badu in the Duyfken
- **1606**: Luis Vaz de Torres navigated through the strait
- **1792**: Captain William Bligh named the island "Mulgrave Island" during his second Pacific breadfruit voyage
- **June 1846**: Three Europeans from the Thomas Lord were killed by Badu Islanders

### The Coming of the Light and Christianisation

The London Missionary Society's arrival at Erub on 1 July 1871 began Christianity's spread through the Torres Strait. After earlier failed attempts, Badu residents welcomed missionaries in 1884. Adoption of Christianity ended warfare and headhunting but was embraced alongside traditional customs (Ailan Kastom).

### Pearl Shell Industry

The pearl shell and beche-de-mer industries reshaped Badu's economy:

| Period | Development |
|--------|------------|
| 1868 | First commercial pearling station in Torres Strait (Warrior Island) |
| 1870s | Pearlers established bases on Badu |
| Early 1880s | Islanders became dependent on pearling wages; by 1883, 200+ pearling vessels operated from the region |
| 1884 | Two pearling stations operating on Badu |
| Late 1890s | Rev. Walker established Papuan Industries Limited (PIL), promoting cooperative lugger ownership |
| 1904 | PIL headquarters opened at Badu |
| 1906 | Islanders purchased first company boat |
| 1930 | Queensland Government took over PIL (renamed Aboriginal Industries Board) |
| Peak (late 1950s) | Badu fleet: 13 boats, 200 workers. Nona family controlled 8 vessels, 50%+ of fleet catch |
| 1950s onward | Plastic buttons replaced pearl shell; industry collapsed |

The pearling industry attracted workers from across Asia and the Pacific, including Japanese, Malay, Filipino, and Pacific Islander divers. The Torres Strait supplied over 50% of the world's pearl shell at the industry's peak in the 1890s.

### Papuan Industries Limited (PIL)

PIL was a unique cooperative enterprise promoting Islander ownership of pearl luggers. Headquartered at Badu from 1904, it represented an early attempt at Indigenous economic self-determination. Coconut plantations were also established, employing both local and South Sea Islander workers. PIL was taken over by the Queensland Government in July 1930 and became the Aboriginal Industries Board.

### Government Control

- **November 1912**: 22,000 acres at Badu gazetted as an Aboriginal reserve
- **1897-1939**: Queensland increasingly restricted Islander movement, labour, and savings under the Aboriginal Protection and Restriction of the Sale of Opium Act 1897
- **1939**: Torres Strait Islanders Act formally recognised Islanders as separate from Aboriginal Australians
- **1939-1950**: Seventeen documented removals from Badu to mainland reserves

### The 1936 Strike

Approximately 70% of the Torres Strait Islander workforce struck for nine months, the first organised challenge to government authority. Demands included wage recognition, trade autonomy, curfew removal, elimination of inter-island travel permits, and crew recruitment rights. About 30 men were imprisoned on Badu. The strike succeeded in removing Protector J.D. McLean, replaced by Cornelius O'Leary, who established consultations with elected Island Council representatives.

### World War II

During WWII, Badu hosted the **341 radar station** monitoring Japanese aircraft movements. Torres Strait Islander men served in the Torres Strait Light Infantry Battalion, initially receiving one-third of white servicemen's pay. After a strike on 31 December 1943, pay increased to two-thirds. Full back pay was not provided until the 1980s.

After the war, the Aboriginal Industries Board headquarters relocated from Badu to Thursday Island. The decline of pearling permitted Islander settlement on Thursday Island and the mainland.

### Native Title Determination (2014)

On **1 February 2014**, the Queensland Government handed over freehold title to **9,836 hectares (24,310 acres)** of land to the Badulgal traditional owners, ending a struggle for recognition dating back to 1939. The **Mura Badulgal (Torres Strait Islanders) Corporation** administers land on behalf of the Badulgal people as a registered native title body corporate.

### Torres Strait Treaty

Papua New Guinea's 1975 independence led to the Torres Strait Treaty (signed December 1978, operational February 1985). The treaty established a Protected Zone allowing free movement for traditional fishing, trading, and family gatherings without passports.

### Local Governance

- **30 March 1985**: Badu elected three councillors forming the autonomous Badu Island Council under the Community Services (Torres Strait) Act 1984
- **21 October 1985**: Former Aboriginal reserve transferred to council trusteeship via Deed of Grant in Trust
- **2008**: Local council merged into the Torres Strait Island Regional Council (15 councillors and a mayor)

## Infrastructure and Services

### St Mark's Church

Constructed between 1933 and 1935 under the direction of Tanu Nona. The church accommodates approximately 700 people and remains in active use. Its construction during the Depression, funded entirely by Islanders through pearling income and voluntary labour, was considered a remarkable achievement.

### Education

Badu Island State School opened on 29 January 1905. On 1 January 2007, it became the **Badu Island campus of Tagai State College**, serving Early Childhood through Year 6. Secondary education requires travelling to Thursday Island or using distance education.

### Badu Art Centre

The **Badhulgaw Kuthinaw Mudh Art Centre** displays and sells local artwork, featuring internationally recognised artists including Alick Tipoti and Laurie Nona. The centre preserves significant cultural artefacts, provides skills training, and has brought the island international recognition for its linocut prints with bold imagery drawn from marine life, ancestral stories, winds, and constellations.

## Notable People

- **Tanu Nona** (c.1902-1980): Pearler, politician, OBE 1972. Born on Saibai, dominated Badu politics from 1929 to 1976. Directed church construction. In 1959, the Nona family controlled eight government vessels.
- **Jacob Baira**: Represented Badu at the first Inter Islander Councillors Conference in 1937
- **Alick Tipoti** (born 1975): Internationally recognised artist, linguist, and cultural activist
- **Laurie Nona**: Internationally recognised artist
- **Ethel May Eliza Zahel** (1877-1951): Teacher and public servant who worked on Badu
- **David Fifita, Dane Gagai, Brenko Lee, Edrick Lee, Tallisha Harden, Treymain Spry**: Sporting figures of Badulgal heritage
