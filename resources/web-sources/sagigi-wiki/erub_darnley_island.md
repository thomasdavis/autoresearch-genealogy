# Erub (Darnley Island)

**Erub**, known in English as Darnley Island, is a volcanic island in the eastern Torres Strait, Queensland, Australia. It is one of the homeland islands of the **Meriam people** and holds profound historical significance as the site of the **Coming of the Light** on 1 July 1871, the event that introduced Christianity to the Torres Strait.

The Doolah family has connections to Erub.

## Geography

Erub is a basaltic island formed by volcanic action, located in the eastern section of the Torres Strait at coordinates 9 degrees 35 minutes S, 143 degrees 46 minutes E. Key geographic facts:

- **Area**: 5.7 square kilometres
- **Distance from Thursday Island**: Approximately 208 kilometres northeast
- **Distance from Cairns**: Approximately 842 kilometres north
- **Formation**: Volcanic origin

The island was named by Captain William Bligh in 1792 during his second breadfruit voyage to the Pacific, after his distant relative, the Earl of Darnley. The name "Erub" comes from the native Papuan language of Meriam Mir.

## The Coming of the Light (1 July 1871)

Erub is the site of the most significant event in Torres Strait Islander religious history. On 1 July 1871, representatives of the **London Missionary Society** arrived aboard the schooner Surprise after the French Government had demanded their removal from the Loyalty Islands and New Caledonia in 1869.

The delegation included:
- **Reverend Samuel Macfarlane** (English)
- **Reverend Archibald Wright Murray** (English)
- **Eight Lifu (Loyalty Islander) evangelists and their wives**

Tribal elder **Dabad** played a crucial role in welcoming the missionaries and facilitating Christianity's introduction to the island. Rev. Macfarlane presented the Bible to Dabad, who accepted the gift, interpreted as the "Light."

The Coming of the Light is celebrated annually on **1 July** across all Torres Strait communities. It represents not merely a religious conversion but a coming together of clans and cultures, a strengthening of family and community, and the beginning of greater cooperation across the region. Torres Strait Islanders adopted Christian rituals and ceremonies while continuing to practise their traditional customs and cultural identity (Ailan Kastom).

## Language

The traditional language of Erub is **Meriam Mir**, classified within the Eastern Trans-Fly language family. It is the only Papuan language on Australian territory. In practice, many residents now speak **Brokan** (Torres Strait Creole) as their community language, though Meriam Mir remains spoken.

## Pearl Diving and Beche-de-Mer Industry

Pearl divers and beche-de-mer (sea cucumber) harvesters frequented Erub from the mid-19th century onward. The industries attracted an influx of seamen from:

- The Pacific Islands
- The Philippines
- Malaya (Malaysia)

Many of these workers married local women and settled on the island, contributing to Erub's multicultural heritage.

The Torres Strait pearl diving industry was notoriously dangerous. The **Darnley Deep**, located near Erub, was a particularly hazardous diving site at 40 fathoms (240 feet / 73 metres). Attacks of the bends (decompression sickness) were common and deaths frequent at such depths.

By the 1890s, the Torres Strait islands were supplying more than 50% of the world's pearl shells. The industry declined in the mid-20th century as plastic buttons replaced pearl shell.

## History

### Mission Period

After the Coming of the Light in 1871, the LMS established a permanent mission presence. Reverend Samuel MacFarlane established the Papuan Industrial School around 1879, teaching religious instruction and trades including boat building.

### All Saints Church (1919)

All Saints Church was built at the original mission site using locally produced lime (from burnt coral) and basalt. Construction was directed by:
- **Manai** (Erub Islander)
- **Ware** (South Sea Islander)

The church is now heritage-listed and remains an important community landmark.

### Government Control

The Queensland Government established schools, medical services, post offices, and an Island Industries Board store on Erub in the early 20th century. As across the Torres Strait, the government increasingly controlled Islander movement, labour, and finances.

### Indigenous Rights Activism

Erub produced several prominent Indigenous rights advocates:

- **George Mye**: Emerged as a prominent Indigenous rights advocate from the 1960s through the 1990s
- **Carlemo Wacando**: Challenged the terra nullius doctrine

### The Mabo Connection

While the Mabo case (1982-1992) was specifically about Mer (Murray Island), it was brought by Meriam people, the same cultural group that inhabits Erub. The landmark High Court decision of 3 June 1992 recognised native title for the Meriam people and, by extension, for all Indigenous Australians.

### Native Title

The **Native Title Act 1993** followed the Mabo decision. On Erub, **Pau Enterprises Indigenous Corporation** was established in 2015 to manage native title lands on behalf of the traditional owners.

## Education

Darnley Island State School opened on 29 January 1985. On 1 January 2007, it became the **Darnley Campus of Tagai State College**, serving Early Childhood through Year 6. Secondary students must travel over 200 kilometres to Thursday Island or mainland schools for further education.

In 2015, the school principal's Facebook appeal for book donations received over 18,000 shares and hundreds of donated books.

## Cultural Significance

Erub occupies a unique position in Torres Strait Islander identity as the landing place of the Coming of the Light. The annual 1 July celebration draws Torres Strait Islanders from across Australia back to the islands, making it one of the most important cultural events in the Torres Strait calendar.

The island's heritage as both a Meriam homeland and the site of first Christian contact creates a dual cultural significance: it embodies both the ancient traditions of the Meriam people and the transformative moment when Christianity was integrated into Torres Strait Islander life.
