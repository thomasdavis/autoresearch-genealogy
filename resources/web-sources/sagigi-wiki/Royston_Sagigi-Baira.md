# Royston Sagigi-Baira

## Identity

Royston Sagigi-Baira (also known as Royston Noell) is a Thanakwith (Aboriginal) and Wagadagam (Torres Strait Islander) man from Mapoon, Queensland. Winner of Australian Idol Season 8 (2023).

## Heritage and Family

- **Mother's heritage:** Thanakwith people. From Mapoon, near Weipa, Cape York Peninsula, Far North Queensland.
- **Father's heritage:** Wagadagam people. From Badu Island in the Torres Strait.
- Grew up in Mapoon, a small Aboriginal settlement near Weipa with approximately 60 students at his school.
- None of his family or siblings sing; he was isolated in his musical passion as a child.
- Was bullied for singing as a child, being the only one in his school interested in music.

## Early Life and Education

- In Year 2, a teacher gave him a High School Musical DVD as a prize; he "watched it over and over again and completely fell in love with music, especially singing."
- Sang in church and at school growing up.
- Attended the Aboriginal Centre for the Performing Arts (ACPA) in Brisbane.
- Studied a Bachelor of Business course at CQUniversity before pivoting to music.
- As of 2023, was in his second year of a Bachelor of Fine Arts (Music) at Queensland University of Technology (QUT).

## Australian Idol 2023

- Auditioned with "I'm Not the Only One" by Sam Smith, receiving a golden ticket to the Top 50.
- Advanced through Top 50, Top 24, Top 12 live shows, Top 10, Top 8, Top 6, and into the Grand Finale (Final 3).
- Performed Whitney Houston's "When You Believe" in the finale.
- Crowned the winner of Australian Idol 2023 on 26 March 2023.
- Won $100,000 prize money and a recording contract with Sony Music Australia.
- Became the second First Nations winner of Australian Idol, after Gumbaynggirr and Dungari woman Casey Donovan (Season 2).
- Identified as a queer Indigenous artist.

## Music Career

- November 2023: Released single "Dreaming."
- November 2024: Released single "Feeling Good."
- Announced debut EP titled "Sunrise."

## Significance

Royston's win was widely celebrated as a milestone for First Nations representation in Australian music. He has spoken about wanting to inspire his community and make change through his platform.
