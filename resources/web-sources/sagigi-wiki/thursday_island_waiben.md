# Thursday Island (Waiben)

**Thursday Island**, colloquially known as **TI** or by its traditional name **Waiben** (in the Kawrareg dialect, also written Waibene), is the administrative and commercial hub of the Torres Strait. Located approximately 39 kilometres north of Cape York Peninsula in Far North Queensland, it is one of the most culturally diverse communities in Australia, shaped by over 150 years of the pearl diving industry that drew workers from Japan, Malaya, the Philippines, the Pacific Islands, China, and India.

## Geography

- **Area**: 3.5 square kilometres
- **Highest point**: Milman Hill (104 metres), site of a World War II defensive facility
- **Location**: Part of the Torres Strait Islands archipelago, comprising at least 274 small islands
- **Climate**: Tropical savanna with wet season (December-April) and dry season (May-November). Temperatures consistently hot year-round (averages: 29.0C in July to 32.1C in November). Annual rainfall averages 1,791.6mm.

Languages spoken include Torres Strait Creole (dominant), Kalaw Lagaw Ya (Mabuiag dialect), Kaiwaligau Ya, and English.

## History

### Naming

Captain Owen Stanley named the island in 1848 (originally called Friday Island). The name was changed to Thursday Island in 1855 by Admiral Francis Beaufort.

### Establishment as Administrative Centre

In 1877, the Queensland Government established an administrative centre for the Torres Strait Islands on Thursday Island, replacing the earlier settlement at Somerset on Cape York. Port Kennedy was established in 1867. The town was officially renamed Thursday Island on 1 June 1962.

### Indigenous Heritage

Melanesian occupation of the Torres Strait region began approximately 2,600 years ago based on archaeological evidence. The Kawrareg people are the traditional owners of Thursday Island and its surrounding waters.

## The Pearl Diving Industry

The pearl diving industry defined Thursday Island's character and created its multicultural heritage.

### Timeline

| Period | Development |
|--------|------------|
| 1868 | First commercial pearling station in Torres Strait (Warrior Island) |
| 1871 | Experimental diving suit by Siebe and Gorman introduced, allowing diving to 15 fathoms |
| 1877 | Administrative centre established on Thursday Island |
| 1883 | Over 200 pearling vessels based on the island |
| 1884 | Formal pearling industry established, attracting Asian workers |
| 1890s | Torres Strait supplied over 50% of the world's pearl shell |
| 1899 | Cyclone Mahina devastated the fleet sheltering in Bathurst Bay |
| 1920s | Almost all pearl divers working around the Inner Islands were Japanese |
| 1942 | Industry disrupted by WWII; luggers requisitioned by Australian Navy |
| 1950s | Plastic buttons replaced pearl shell; industry entered terminal decline |
| 1970s | Cultured pearl farming attempts devastated by disease |

### The Product

The industry focused on harvesting **Golden Lip Oysters** for pearl shell buttons and decorative items. Trochus shell was also harvested using specialized two-masted luggers. Divers worked in varying depths, with the most dangerous sites reaching 40 fathoms (240 feet).

### Japanese Divers

Japanese workers formed the backbone of the pearling industry for decades:

- Between the 1880s and 1942, approximately **6,000 Japanese divers** worked in the Torres Strait
- By the 1920s, almost all pearl divers working around the Inner Islands were Japanese
- Japanese residents included indentured divers, boat hands, and longer-term settlers involved in boat building and lugger ownership
- Some engaged in "dummying" (unofficial vessel ownership arrangements)
- The **Japanese Cemetery** on Thursday Island contains approximately 700 graves, a poignant reminder of the industry's human cost

### Other Communities

The pearl industry attracted diverse communities:

- **Malay workers**: Skilled divers and boat crew
- **Filipino workers**: Divers and maritime labourers
- **Pacific Islanders**: Some originally brought through blackbirding (coercive labour practices)
- **Indian workers**: Arrived seeking fortune in the pearling trade
- **Chinese community**: Established businesses and services

This multicultural legacy gives Thursday Island a demographic character unlike anywhere else in Australia.

## World War II

Thursday Island served as military headquarters for Torres Strait operations during WWII:

- Australian and U.S. forces were stationed on and around the island
- **January 1942**: Civilian evacuation ordered
- Residents of Japanese descent were **interned** during the war
- Japanese residents were largely **repatriated** after the war ended
- Thursday Island itself was spared bombing, possibly because it was the burial place of many Japanese pearl shell divers
- Neighbouring **Horn Island** experienced extensive bombing by Japanese aircraft

The **Torres Strait Light Infantry Battalion**, composed almost entirely of Torres Strait Islander men, was part of the Torres Strait Force defending the islands.

### Military Infrastructure

A fort was constructed at **Battery Point** in 1892 due to fears of Russian invasion during British-Russian imperial tensions. It remained operational until 1927. During WWII, **Milman Hill** (the island's highest point) was developed as a defensive facility.

## Administrative Role

Thursday Island serves as the seat of government and administration for the Torres Strait region:

- **Torres Strait Island Regional Council**: Administers the outer islands (Thursday Island itself sits within the Shire of Torres but serves as the administrative hub)
- **State electorate**: Cook
- **Federal division**: Leichhardt
- Government offices, courts, and services are concentrated on the island

### Ports and Transport

- **Ports North** operates wharves on Thursday Island and nearby Horn Island
- **Torres Strait Pilots**: A cooperative of Master Mariners navigating ships through complex reef systems
- Horn Island Airport serves as the primary air link to the mainland (Cairns)
- Regular ferry services connect Thursday Island to surrounding islands

## Heritage Sites

- **Green Hill Fort**: Military heritage
- **Quetta Memorial Precinct**: Commemorating the 1890 RMS Quetta disaster (over 130 lives lost), including the Quetta All Souls Memorial Cathedral
- **Our Lady of the Sacred Heart Church**
- **Thursday Island Cemetery** (incorporating the Japanese Cemetery with ~700 graves)
- **1938 Customs House**

## Cultural Facilities

- **Gab Titui Cultural Centre** (opened 2004): Showcases Torres Strait Islander heritage and contemporary artworks
- Libraries and sporting facilities
- Tagai State College (primary and secondary campuses)
- Our Lady of the Sacred Heart School (Catholic primary)
- Torres Strait Campus of Tropical North Queensland TAFE

## Economy

The contemporary economy relies on:
- Government administration
- Pearl farming (reduced from historical scale)
- Commercial fishing
- Tourism
- Maritime services (pilotage, ports)

Two wind turbines provide partial electricity generation. Water is partially piped from adjacent islands.

## Communications

The **Paterson Telegraph Station** was connected to Thursday Island via undersea cable in 1887, linking the Torres Strait to the Australian telegraph network. The island became a regular port for Australia-Southeast Asia trading vessels.

## Notable People

- **Henry Gibson "Seaman" Dan**: Musician
- **John Douglas**: Premier of Queensland (1877-1879), later Government Resident in Torres Strait
- **Bernard Namok**: Designer of the Torres Strait Islander flag
- **Elma Gada Kris**: Dancer
- **The Mills Sisters**: Musical group
- **Danny Morseu**: Basketball player
- **Tiarna Ernst**: AFLW player

## Popular Culture

Thursday Island served as a filming location for:
- Lovers and Luggers (1937)
- King of the Coral Sea (1954)

Ion Idriess's novel The Wild White Man of Badu (1950) also drew attention to the Torres Strait region.
