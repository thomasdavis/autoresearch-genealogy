# Jack Noble / Wannamutta: Full NMP Service Record

## Identity

- Birth name: Wannamutta
- Also known as: Jack Noble, Jack Moran/Morran, Nobel Morran
- Born: c.1855, Sandy Cape, K'gari (Fraser Island)
- Died: 15 January 1935, Pialba, kidney disease, aged approximately 80
- People: Butchulla (Budjula)
- Parents: Jacko Morris and Carie Martin
- Acquired surname "Noble" during Victorian service, reportedly given for his tracking efforts

## Recruitment

Aged 14, removed from family by Constable Thomas King on K'gari and enlisted into the Queensland Native Mounted Police (NMP).

## NMP Service Postings (Chronological)

1. Rockhampton: initial training under Constable Thomas King
2. October 1873: traveled north aboard SS Leichhardt, arriving Cooktown 23 October
3. 5 November 1873: present at Normanby River lagoon massacre (Battle Camp), where 80 to 150 Kuku-Warra people were killed
4. 1875: stationed at Boralga, Laura River, under Sub-Inspector Stanhope O'Connor
5. 1879: Lower Laura
6. February 1879: traveled to Victoria with five fellow troopers (Hero, Johnny, Jimmy, Barney, Corporal Sambo) under Sub-Inspector O'Connor and Senior Constable Thomas King for Kelly Gang pursuit
7. 28 June 1880: present at Siege of Glenrowan
8. 1886: Dunrobin
9. 1889: Turn Off Lagoon (Corinda) Police Station near Burketown
10. 1899-1901: Turn Off Lagoon
11. 1902: Musgrave

## Kelly Gang Pursuit and Glenrowan

In February 1879, Jack and five fellow troopers traveled to Victoria:
- Hero
- Johnny
- Jimmy
- Barney
- Corporal Sambo (died 19 March 1879 in Melbourne from lung inflammation)

Under Sub-Inspector Stanhope O'Connor and Senior Constable Thomas King.

On 27 June 1880, after Aaron Sherritt's murder, the party left Melbourne by special train. At the Siege of Glenrowan (28 June 1880), the troopers positioned in a creek near the hotel and fired on the building. Kelly gang members called them "little black devils."

Jack and the surviving troopers were promised 50 pounds each from the 8,000 pound reward. Descendants state this was never paid. A 1994 descendant claim for $42 million (inflation-adjusted) failed; the court ruled the Queensland government received the funds on their behalf.

## Turn Off Lagoon Incident

At Turn Off Lagoon (Corinda) Police Station near Burketown (1889), Jack tracked escaped prisoner Joe Flick to Lawn Hill station. A gunfight ensued that killed Constable Wavell and an Aboriginal stockman. Jack left the NMP after this incident.

## Post-NMP Life

- Returned to K'gari after leaving the NMP (c.1889)
- At Bogimbah Mission (early 1900s), married Alberta Clift, a Quandamooka (Jandai) woman from Stradbroke Island
- Customarily adopted Alberta's son George, who took the surname Noble
- Worked at Emu Park in 1902 before returning to K'gari with George and Jack's father

## Mission Removals

- 3 October 1902: Jack and Alberta removed to Yarrabah Mission near Cairns under Ernest Gribble
- Conflicts with Gribble's wife Emilie led them to leave for Cairns, but Gribble prevented George from joining
- 1903: returned to Yarrabah. Sheltered mission absconders
- April 1906: moved to Myora Reserve, Stradbroke Island; George remained at Yarrabah
- Alberta wrote pleading letters for her son's return
- 1926 and 1928: Jack removed to Barambah (Cherbourg) Aboriginal Settlement

## Death

15 January 1935 at Pialba, kidney disease. Predeceased by Alberta. Survived by George.

Note: burial location shows discrepancy. ADB says "buried Polson Cemetery, Pialba." Frontier Conflict database says "Poison Cemetery, Dunwich." These may be the same cemetery with variant spellings, or a genuine discrepancy requiring resolution.

## Associate

Willie Wundunna: fellow tracker, same recruitment period, also resided at Pialba.

## Significance to Sandra Anne Davis Research

Jack Noble / Wannamutta is Sandra Anne Davis's maternal great-grandfather through the line:
Jack Noble > George Noble (adopted) > George Clifford Noble > Dorothy Molly Noble > Sandra Anne Davis

George Noble (adopted) married Molly at Yarrabah. They were removed to Mona Mona in 1914 with child George (later George Gregory Noble). Their daughter Dorothy Molly Noble married Charles Joseph "Chooka" Davis in 1935.
