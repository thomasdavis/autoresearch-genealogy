# Torres Strait Family Networks: Sagigi, Doolah, and Cowley

Date: 2026-04-10

## Summary

Research into connections between the Sagigi, Doolah, and Cowley families in the Torres Strait. All three surnames appear in historical government records from the Torres Strait region. The Sagigi family is strongly associated with Badu Island (western Torres Strait), the Doolah family with Darnley Island/Erub and Murray Island/Mer (eastern Torres Strait), and the Cowley family with the Torres Strait generally (likely Thursday Island). A key connecting node is the Doolah to Bin Juda marriage, which links the eastern islands Doolah family to Cherbourg mission on the mainland.

No direct cross-family marriage or parent-child relationships between these three specific families were found in publicly accessible records. However, they overlap geographically and appear together in multiple government administrative records from the 1940s and 1950s.

## Family Profiles

### Sagigi Family (Badu Island, western Torres Strait)

**Island Origin:** Badu Island (primary), with secondary connections to Murray Island/Mer and Mabuiag Island.

**1941 Child Endowment Records (CIFHS):**
- Baita Sagigi TSI (Badu Island): children Nazareth (b. 1.10.1934), Natanelu (b. 19.4.1937), Masi (b. 13.7.1938), Kathleen (b. 10.9.1926), Seilu (b. 5.10.1930), Margaret (b. 1933)
- Gawagi Sagigi TSI (Badu Island): children Rosa (b. 16.3.1938), Diat (b. 12.10.1939)

**NAA J1781 Records:**
- Sagigi, Salepata (listed in J1781 combined records)
- Sagigi, Samuel (listed)
- Sagigi, Charles (listed)

**1928 Aboriginals Survey:**
- Sagigi, aged 12, diagnosed with Hookworm

**Deaths Records (QLD BDM):**
- Napthali Namok, d. 17 Dec 1961 (1961/C/5025), parents: Rasa Sagigi and William Namok
- Neira Namok, d. 15 Oct 1975 (1975/C/6229), parents: Rasa Sagigi and William Namok
- Irene R Giblet, d. 3 Oct 1985 (1985/57427), born 1944, parents: Rasa Irene Sagigi and William Namok

**Find a Grave:**
- Jacob Dan Sagigi Jr. (2 Jun 1959 to 21 May 2001), Cairns Cemetery
- Maria Sagigi Baira (1914 to 12 Dec 1987), Cairns Cemetery

**Native Title:** Dan Sagigi (Snr), traditional Badulaig elder, was the original applicant in the Badu Island native title claim. Died July 2002. Victor Nona became named applicant after his death. Native title determined 1 February 2014.

**Surname Distribution:** Approximately 84 bearers in Australia (forebears.io), 95% in Queensland, 5% in NSW. Also 2 bearers in Papua New Guinea.

### Doolah Family (Darnley Island/Erub and Murray Island/Mer, eastern Torres Strait)

**Island Origin:** Darnley Island (Erub) primary, with connections to Murray Island (Mer) and Badu Island.

**1941 Child Endowment Records (CIFHS):**
- Charlotte Doolah TSI (Darnley Island): children Rene (b. 10.12.1925), Reuben (b. 12.6.1927), Colina (b. 22.5.1929), Geoffrey (b. 8.6.1931), Richard (b. 14.9.1935), Taibi Napoleon (b. 15.3.1939)
- Abakee Doolah (Murray Island): listed among claimants

**QPP Records:**
- Doolah, Jeffrey [101909], listed in Queensland Parliamentary Papers

**Thursday Island Audit (1946, QSA):**
- Doolah: listed in Thursday Island audit
- Jeffrey Doolah: Thursday Island, account overdrawn

**TB Records (1951):**
- Taite Doolah, Male, 11 yrs, Darnley Island (patient in Thursday Island/Waiben Hospitals, 3 July 1951)
- Sam Doolah, Male, 14 yrs, Darnley Island (patient in Thursday Island/Waiben Hospitals, 3 July 1951)

**Key Marriage: Doolah to Bin Juda**
- Bora Bin Juda (b. 1895, Macassar/Makassar, Indonesia) married Mareja Doolah (b. 1902, Badu Island) on 29 December 1922. They were living at Cherbourg with four children. Bora worked there until breaking his arm. In January 1943 he left to look for work; his wife remained at Cherbourg.
- Source: Julia Martinez, "Aboriginal History," ANU Press, Volume 35, 2011

**Note on Mareja Doolah's origin:** The Martinez source states Mareja Doolah was born on Badu Island in 1902, creating a link between the Doolah family (primarily associated with Darnley/Erub in child endowment records) and Badu Island (the Sagigi family's home island). This suggests either the Doolah family had branches on multiple islands, or there was inter-island movement/marriage.

**Descendant: Leilani Bin-Juda**
- Leilani Bin-Juda is a descendant of the Bin Juda/Doolah line
- Torres Strait Islander heritage with ties to Hammond, Darnley and Murray Islands
- Great-grandfathers from Japan, China, Philippines, Indonesia, Jamaica and Niue; great-grandmothers from various Torres Strait islands
- CEO of Torres Strait Regional Authority
- First Torres Strait Islander at DFAT Torres Strait Treaty Liaison Office
- Appointed Australia's High Commissioner to Nigeria in November 2023, the first Indigenous Australian in that role
- Source: Indigenous Diplomacy CDU, DFAT, ANZSOG

**Geoffrey Doolah at Cherbourg:**
- Geoffrey Doolah (b. 8.6.1931 per child endowment) was involved in setting up Aboriginal arts and crafts and shell displays for the Native Affairs Department at Cherbourg Aboriginal Community in 1958
- Taught at Cherbourg Mission State School in 1959
- Performed banjo at Cherbourg concert (1953)
- Photographed at Boys Dormitory, Barambah Aboriginal Settlement c.1929
- Source: Cherbourg Memory Project, ANU Press

**Dr. John Doolah:**
- Heritage from eastern islands of Torres Strait: Erub (Darnley Island) and Mer (Murray Island)
- Sager people of Mer, clan division Samsep-Meriam
- Family migrated from Torres Strait to mainland Australia in the 1960s
- First postgraduate Torres Strait Islander student at University of Newcastle to graduate with Masters in Aboriginal Studies (2015)
- PhD thesis: "Decolonising the migration and urbanisation of Torres Strait Islanders (Ailan pipel) from the Torres Straits to mainland Australia between the 1960s and 1970s"
- Currently Ngarrngga Postdoctoral Fellow and lecturer, University of Melbourne
- "Nene Charlotte Buri Doolah" (his grandmother) documented in thesis
- Source: University of Newcastle, ResearchGate, University of Melbourne

**Surname Distribution:** Approximately 27 bearers in Australia (forebears.io), 96% in Queensland, 4% in NSW.

**Find a Grave:**
- Hezakai Rapana Doolah (b. 23 Nov 2010), Great Southern Garden of Remembrance, Carbrook, Logan City, Queensland

**Thursday Island Audit (1946, QSA):**

- Tom Cowley: deceased
- Tassie Cowley: deceased

**Other Government Records:**

**Deaths Records:**

- No Find a Grave results for Cowley on Thursday Island

## Geographic Overlap

All three families appear in Thursday Island administrative records from the 1940s:
- **Sagigi:** Badu Island (child endowment), J1781 records
- **Doolah:** Darnley Island (child endowment), Thursday Island audit (Jeffrey Doolah, account overdrawn)
- **Cowley:** Thursday Island audit (Leo Cowley listed)

The Doolah and Sagigi families share a connection to Badu Island through Mareja Doolah (b. 1902, Badu Island).

## Key Connecting Points

1. **Badu Island** is a shared geography: the Sagigi family's home island, and where Mareja Doolah (who married Bora Bin Juda) was born
2. **Thursday Island** serves as the administrative hub where all three families appear in government records
3. **Cherbourg Mission** on the mainland received Torres Strait Islanders including Geoffrey Doolah (who was from Darnley Island originally) and the Bin Juda/Doolah family
4. **The Baira surname** appears in connection with both families: Maria Sagigi Baira (d. 1987), Simi Baira (in TI audit), and Royston Sagigi-Baira

## Potential Further Research

1. **Margaret Lawrie Collection (State Library of Queensland):** The genealogy sheets for Badu, Darnley Island, Murray Island, and Thursday Island communities (1964-1973) are the most likely source of definitive family connections. Access is restricted but available to family members and researchers.
2. **Queensland BDM Online Index:** Search for Cowley births/deaths/marriages in Torres Strait district, particularly for George Cowley and Balo Boa's marriage, and Leo William Cowley's death (1938).
3. **Queensland State Archives:** Protection Act records for all three families. The Community and Personal Histories Team offers free research services for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people.
4. **AIATSIS Biographical Index:** Over 45,000 names; search for all three surnames and variants.
5. **Ancestry.com family trees:** The Leo William Cowley record suggests at least one public family tree exists; this could contain further connections.
6. **NAA (National Archives of Australia):** J1781 series and other Torres Strait records may contain further details on family relationships.
7. **John Doolah's thesis and publications:** His doctoral and post-doctoral work on Torres Strait Islander migration may contain detailed family genealogy. Available through University of Newcastle repository.
